Education Across Australia

Let’s dive into the 2021 Australian education sphere to find out what influences how people are educated and what they are choosing to study.

Who is Graduating from University?



Looking at which states have the highest percentage of people with bachelor's degrees, we see a variation of about 10%. The largest difference is approximately 9% between the ACT and NT.

Even more interesting is how this data correlates with other factors. For example, the most densely populated states are the ACT, followed by Victoria, and then NSW.

This suggests that factors like population density can significantly influence the type of education pursued by everyday Australians.



Education Across the Ages



This graph highlights both the variation and consistency in what different age groups choose to study. Fields like Engineering and Management/Commerce vary dramatically with age, while fields like Architecture and Science remain extremely consistent.

These trends can also be linked to real-life influences. For example, Education peaks at ages 25-29 for recent graduates, and again at 65-69 for those retiring from their main careers and taking up substitute teaching.

This graph shows how age affects the likelihood of graduating from high school. The younger you are, the more likely you are to finish Year 12, and the less likely you are to not attend school. In fact, only 0.11% of 15-18-year-olds didn’t attend school, while more than 3.4% of those aged 85+ did not attend high school.



How Does Gender Influence Your Choices?



As we can see, there is a significant variation in the fields chosen by different genders. Fields like Architecture and IT are heavily male-dominated, while areas like Education and Health are dominated by women. The greatest difference, however, is in Engineering, where across all age groups, almost 92% of qualified engineers are male.

If we investigate the percentage of women in Engineering across age groups, we can see that more women are studying Engineering in younger generations.

However, even at its peak among 30-34-year-olds, only 11.86% of engineers are women.



Where Do Our Choices Come From?



Some states seem to specialize in one or two areas. For example, WA focuses on Engineering, while the ACT specializes in IT, Science, and Society. Other states like Victoria and NSW, which have the most universities, show a more even distribution across different fields.

Conclusion



As we can see, there is significant variation in both the level of education received and the fields of study chosen. These choices can be heavily influenced by factors such as location, age, and gender. This diversity contributes to the rich and varied culture of the Australian people, creating an incredibly diverse educational landscape.
